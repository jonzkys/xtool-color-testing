When you raster a grayscale depth map, the machine turns each pixel's
brightness into a number of laser passes — brighter is deeper. That makes
the engraving exquisitely sensitive to the *input*: a single noisy pixel
becomes a pit, a sensor's pepper noise becomes a rough rind, and a gentle
slope that crosses a pass-count threshold aliases into a visible terrace.
**Relief** cleans the heightfield before the machine ever sees it.

Drop in a depth map and Relief runs an edge-aware smooth on the backend:

- **A median pass** kills single-pixel spikes — the oscillation that reads
  as grit on the finished surface.
- **A bilateral smooth** fairs the gentle regions without bleeding across
  real boundaries.
- **A guard rail** (on by default) freezes anything steeper than a
  threshold you set, so a *legitimate* sharp drop stays sharp while the
  noise around it melts away. Turn it off and watch hard edges soften —
  the difference is the whole point.

You drive it live: a **strength** slider, the **edge-preserve** threshold,
and **speckle removal**, each re-rendering a downscaled preview in a few
hundred milliseconds (the spatial radius is scaled to match, so the preview
tracks the full-res export). Compare raw vs. cleaned through a **draggable
wipe**, flip to an **orbitable 3D surface** to see the relief the way the
laser will carve it — toggling Original ↔ Cleaned right on the mesh — and
keep an eye on the **inspect strip**: a luminance histogram, a gradient
map of where edges remain, and the percentage of pixels you actually
changed.

When it looks right, **export the cleaned PNG** and send it to the machine.

> Relief lives under **Engraving → Relief**. This is Phase 1 — the
> smoothing and the previews. Emitting a finished `.xcs` relief job
> directly (with the pass-count / Z-descent settings baked in) is the next
> step.
