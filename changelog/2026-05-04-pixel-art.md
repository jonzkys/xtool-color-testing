A new page at **#/pixel-art** turns any raster image — photos, logos,
8-bit art — into a grid of laser-engraved rectangles.

Pick a material; the page locks the crop frame to that material's
aspect ratio. Upload an image, drag the frame to compose, then dial
in the grid: a `cells across` slider linked to a `cell (mm)` field
so you always see both numbers. The minimum cell is twice the active
machine's laser spot, enforced live.

The preview re-pixelates as you adjust. Each cell averages to one of
≤K colours (default 8), quantised in CIE Lab so the buckets match
what your eye sees. Each colour auto-matches the closest entry in
your calibrated palette; tap a row to override, or merge near-
identical colours by similarity %. Disabled colours skip-engrave —
useful for using the material's natural surface as a background.

Each enabled colour becomes one compound SVG path in the export, so
the `.xcs` stays well under XCS's display-element budget no matter how
detailed the image — a 100×100 grid is still K=8 displays, not 10,000.

Export an `.xcs` for XCS Studio with every colour calibrated, and a
paired `.svg` faithful to the pixelation.
